## General explainer (not event claims)
- A station in low Earth orbit flies at roughly 17,000 mph, about 240 miles up (Tiangong about 390 km; NASA/ESA basics).
- A lower orbit is a faster lap, so a chasing spacecraft starts lower and behind, then raises its orbit in steps (phasing / Hohmann transfers). NASA rendezvous basics: https://www.nasa.gov/reference/jsc-rendezvous-proximity-operations-docking-subsystems/
- Launch windows: liftoff is timed for when the launch site passes under the station's orbital path; older profiles took about two days (Soyuz and early Shenzhou flights), fast profiles take hours.
- Reaching orbit takes roughly ten minutes (Xinhua-based coverage of this flight: about 10 minutes; typical for crewed launches).
- Final approach is at walking pace or slower; latches then pull the two craft together and seal them. Shenzhou uses an automated fast rendezvous (Aviation Week on the 3.5-hour profile first flown by Shenzhou-21: https://aviationweek.com/space/space-exploration/shenzhou-21-achieves-chinas-fastest-manned-space-docking).
- Space stations are visible to the naked eye as a bright, steady, fast-moving light near dawn or dusk (NASA Spot the Station: https://spotthestation.nasa.gov/).
