## Voiceover script (9 beats)

### hook
VO: This week, a Chinese spacecraft docked with the Tiangong space station, carrying the first foreign astronaut ever to board it, an astronaut from Pakistan. And it got there about three and a half hours after liftoff.
Visual: Frame 1: close on the capsule's nose locked into the station's docking ring, gold solar wings catching the sun, bright Earth below. Card "TIANGONG SPACE STATION · NOV 2026", chips "FIRST FOREIGN ASTRONAUT ABOARD", "LIFTOFF TO DOCKING: ABOUT 3.5 HOURS". **Hero moment 1:** capsule and station joined above the sunlit blue Earth.

### question
VO: Three and a half hours, to catch a target moving at about seventeen thousand miles an hour. How? Rewind.
Visual: Camera starts backing away from the ring; 2D speed readout "ABOUT 17,000 MPH" on the station. Title card "HOW DO YOU CATCH A SPACE STATION?" then a 2D "◀◀ REWIND" strip.

### rewind_contact
VO: Seconds before contact, the two are lined up nose to port, and the gap is closing slower than you walk. A docking is usually a gentle nudge. Then latches grab hold, and pull the two together until they seal.
Visual: Strip "SECONDS BEFORE". The capsule un-docks in reverse and floats a few metres off the ring; a 2D closing-speed gauge reads "SLOWER THAN WALKING PACE"; small latch icons open around the ring. Label "ILLUSTRATION".

### rewind_chase
VO: Rewind again. An hour or so earlier, the capsule is behind the station, and below it. Here's the trick: a lower orbit is a faster lap. So a chasing spacecraft usually stays low to catch up, then fires its engines to climb.
Visual: Strip "AN HOUR OR SO EARLIER". Camera backs out and drops: two curved 2D orbit lines over the Earth, the station on the higher one, the capsule on the lower, visibly gaining; a short engine puff lifts it a step. Chips "LOWER ORBIT = FASTER LAP", "ENGINE BURN → CLIMB". **Hero moment 2:** wide view of both orbit lines arcing over the bright Earth with the tiny capsule chasing the station.

### rewind_orbit
VO: Rewind further. Roughly ten minutes after liftoff, the rocket's work is done. The capsule is in orbit, on its own.
Visual: Strip "ABOUT 10 MINUTES AFTER LIFTOFF". Camera keeps falling back to the edge of the atmosphere: the capsule separates from the last rocket stage in reverse, solar panels folding; the blue horizon glow fills the frame.

### rewind_pad
VO: And all the way back, to the launch pad. Because the real secret happens before the engines even light. Liftoff is timed for the moment the station's path passes overhead.
Visual: Strip "LIFTOFF". Camera descends through bright sky to the desert pad and the white rocket; high above, a 2D dotted line (the station's path) sweeps across the sky until it sits right over the pad. Chip "LAUNCH WINDOW".

### forward
VO: Miss that window, and the chase could take days instead of hours. Hit it, and computers can fly almost the whole pursuit on their own.
Visual: The rocket lifts off cleanly; fast eased climb back up the same path, the orbit lines and capsule flashing past. 2D compare bars: "MISSED WINDOW: DAYS" versus "ON TIME: HOURS". Chip "AUTOMATED RENDEZVOUS".

### takeaway
VO: Takeaway: you can watch a space station yourself. Look for a bright, steady light, gliding across the sky just after sunset or before sunrise. Free tracking sites will tell you when.
Visual: The climb pauses at the horizon: a tiny 2D silhouette of a person on a hill looking up, a bright dot crossing a dusk-blue band of sky above the sunlit Earth limb. 2D checklist: "BRIGHT", "STEADY, NO BLINKING", "JUST AFTER SUNSET OR BEFORE SUNRISE".

### outro
VO: Would you ride a rocket to a space station if you had the chance? Tell us in the comments, and send this to someone who loves space. Because this week, a three-and-a-half-hour chase ended with a gentle nudge, and a first.
Visual: Camera arrives back at the frame-1 view: the capsule locked to the docking ring, gold wings, bright Earth. Chip "A FIRST". Comment and share icons.
