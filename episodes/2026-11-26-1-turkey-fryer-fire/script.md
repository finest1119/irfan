## Voiceover script (9 beats)

### hook
VO: Thanksgiving is the peak day of the year for home cooking fires in America. That's from the National Fire Protection Association. And it discourages one cooking method: the oil turkey fryer.
Visual: Bright stylised suburban backyard on a crisp autumn morning, a big steel fryer pot on a burner stand in the middle of the lawn, calendar card "THANKSGIVING" flipping in. No fire yet.

### question
VO: So why do turkey fryers go wrong so fast? Here are the three big reasons.
Visual: Slow orbit around the fryer pot with a cartoon turkey hanging on a hook above it; title card "WHY DO TURKEY FRYERS EXPLODE?".

### one_overfill
VO: One: too much oil. A turkey is big. Lower it into a full pot and the oil level rises. It can spill over the rim, straight down onto the open burner underneath. Even a small spill can turn into a large fire.
Visual: Glass cutaway pot: golden oil level climbs as the turkey lowers, a ribbon of oil runs down the side toward a soft orange glow under the pot. Glow pulses abstractly; no realistic flames.

### two_ice
VO: Two: ice and water. A turkey that is still partly frozen is carrying water. When water hits hot oil, it flashes into steam. Steam takes up far more room, so it shoves the oil up and out of the pot.
Visual: Cutaway turkey with blue ice crystals inside; on contact the crystals turn into white puff-spheres that expand and push golden oil droplets up and out like a fountain. Stylised particles only.

### three_heat
VO: Three: runaway heat. Some fryers have no temperature control. Leave the burner on and the oil keeps getting hotter, until it can catch fire.
Visual: Giant friendly thermometer beside the pot, needle sweeping from green through amber to red; oil colour shifts from gold to dark amber with wisps of smoke. No flames.

### metaphor
VO: Think of a bathtub filled to the brim. Step in, and the water has to go somewhere. Now picture that water as hot oil, with a flame under the tub.
Visual: Toy-like bathtub brim-full of blue water; a rubber duck plops in and water sloshes over the edge; the tub morphs into the fryer pot and the water turns gold.

### location
VO: Where you fry matters too. Fire departments warn against using a fryer in a garage or on a wooden deck, where spilled oil can spread a fire to the house.
Visual: Top-down toy house with garage and wooden deck; red "no" rings appear over the garage and the deck, a green ring appears on open lawn well away from the house.

### takeaway
VO: Takeaway: thaw the bird completely and pat it dry. Test the oil level with water first. Fry outside, on level ground, away from anything that burns. Never leave it alone. Or choose an oil-free fryer.
Visual: Clean checklist world: four floating icons tick on in turn (thawed turkey, water-line test, fryer on open lawn, person standing watch), then an oil-free electric fryer slides in.

### outro
VO: Has anyone in your family ever deep-fried a turkey? Tell us how it went in the comments, and send this to whoever is cooking this year. Because Thanksgiving is the peak day for home cooking fires.
Visual: Family table set for Thanksgiving in warm light, golden turkey safely on the table, comment and share icons; calendar card "THANKSGIVING" returns to close the loop.

## Risks / caveats

- **80% figure dropped from the voiceover.** It is supported by one outlet only (Carrier Management, 16 Nov 2026). If the main loop wants it, use it only as an attributed on-screen chip, worded exactly "80% of fires reported on Thanksgiving involved cooking equipment (NFPA, 2020–2024)". It is a share of all fires reported that day, not a share of cooking fires. Do not use the injuries/deaths half of that NFPA sentence.
- "Explode" is in the title/keyword only (matches the calendar topic and common search wording). The script describes boil-over, steam and ignition, not a detonation. Visuals must stay abstract: glow, particles, colour shifts. No realistic flames, no burns, no people in danger, no burned house.
- No oil temperatures, smoke points or steam expansion ratios are stated: none were verified with two sources.
- "Water flashes into steam and shoves the oil out" is basic physics that explains the spill-over fire agencies warn about; the steam mechanism itself was not separately sourced.
- Hook claim 2 (NFPA discourages oil fryers) was read in one 2026 outlet only; it is worded as NFPA's advice. "Completely" was removed from the hook because the source says only "discourages".
- "Some fryers have no temperature control" is deliberately "some", not "most".
- The script is evergreen: it never says "today" or "tomorrow", so it still works if it is watched after Thanksgiving.
- No brands or products named. The NFPA position is reported as its advice, not as a claim that every fryer is unsafe.
- Re-check on posting day that the NFPA "peak day" statement is unchanged (it has been stable for years).
