## Voiceover script (9 beats)

### hook
VO: On Sunday, November eighth, fire broke out in a high-rise of about forty stories in downtown Taiyuan, in northern China. The flames were out in about two hours. The cause is still under investigation.
Visual: Bright stylised Chinese city skyline at late-afternoon golden hour; one generic glass tower in the centre with soft grey smoke wisps drifting from its lower levels and small fire trucks with blue lights at its base. Card "TAIYUAN, CHINA · 8 NOV 2026", label "ILLUSTRATION". No flames, no people at windows.

### question
VO: So why can fire and smoke move so fast through a tall building? Here are the three big reasons.
Visual: The tower turns into a clean doll-house cutaway showing floors, a stair core and elevator shafts; title card "WHY FIRE SPREADS FAST IN TALL BUILDINGS".

### one_chimney
VO: One: the building acts like a chimney. Stairwells, elevator shafts and ducts run from bottom to top. Warm air rises through them and pulls more air in below. Engineers call it the stack effect, and it can carry smoke many floors from the fire.
Visual: Cutaway tower with the vertical shafts highlighted in teal; warm orange arrows flow up the shafts while cool blue arrows are drawn in at the base, and a grey smoke ribbon travels up and spills onto a high floor.

### two_outside
VO: Two: the outside wall. Flames can break out of a window and heat the floor above. Engineers call it leapfrogging. Some exterior cladding can also burn, giving fire a path up the building.
Visual: Simple block-tower facade: an orange glow hops from one window square to the one above like a game piece; then a panel layer peels away to show cladding with a rising colour gradient. Abstract glow only, generic building.

### three_height
VO: Three: height. Fire truck ladders only reach the lower floors. Above that, firefighters climb the stairs with their gear and connect hoses to pipes built into the building. Every floor costs time.
Visual: Toy fire truck extends its ladder against a very tall tower and stops well short, with a dotted line to the top; inside, small firefighter figures climb a stairwell beside a red standpipe.

### smoke
VO: And the biggest danger is often not the flames but the smoke. It can fill hallways and stairwells far from the fire. That is why one closed door matters so much.
Visual: Corridor cutaway: soft grey fog rolls along a hallway ceiling and stops at a closed door; the room behind the door stays clear and bright. Nobody in distress.

### protections
VO: Modern towers fight back: sprinklers, fire doors that close on their own, stairwells kept under pressure to push smoke out, and alarms on every floor.
Visual: Four systems light up one by one in the cutaway tower: blue sprinkler cones, doors swinging shut, a fan pushing clean air into the stair core, alarm bells pulsing.

### takeaway
VO: Takeaway: wherever you stay, count the doors to the nearest stairs. Never take the elevator in a fire. And if smoke blocks your way out, shut the door, seal the gaps and call for help.
Visual: Bright hotel hallway with numbered doors counted off toward a glowing green stair sign; elevator doors with a red "no" ring; a towel placed at the foot of a closed door.

### outro
VO: Do you know where the fire stairs are in your building? Tell us in the comments, and send this to someone who lives or works high up. Investigators in Taiyuan are still looking for a cause. But how fire moves through a tall building is no mystery.
Visual: Return to the golden-hour skyline, the tower now calm with every protection icon softly glowing; comment and share icons; card "TAIYUAN: CAUSE UNDER INVESTIGATION".
