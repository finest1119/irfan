## Voiceover script (10 beats)

### hook
VO: On Tuesday, America's skies had their busiest day in sixteen years: about fifty-two thousand flights, officials said. Two days earlier, one computer system failed at one of the country's biggest airlines, and roughly two thousand of its flights were delayed that day.
Visual: Frame 1: a fully boarded jet at its gate in bright sun, jet bridge attached, not moving; behind it a long row of identical parked jets. Card "USA · THANKSGIVING WEEK 2026", chip "TUE 24 NOV: ABOUT 52,000 FLIGHTS (officials)", then chip "SUN 22 NOV: ABOUT 2,000 OF ONE AIRLINE'S FLIGHTS DELAYED". **Hero moment 1:** the endless row of parked jets at their gates.

### question
VO: Nothing was wrong with the planes. The airline said most of the trouble traced to that one system, and what it does is math. So how can one glitch stall an airline? Watch the dominoes.
Visual: Camera pushes toward the cockpit window: a 2D tablet overlay shows a blank load sheet, "WEIGHT: — · BALANCE: —". Title card "HOW ONE GLITCH CAN STALL AN AIRLINE".

### domino_numbers
VO: Domino one: no numbers. Before any airliner takes off, the crew needs its exact weight and its balance point. Those numbers set the takeoff speed and the engine power. No numbers, no takeoff.
Visual: Camera slides along the fuselage; a 2D see-saw line appears under the jet with a balance-point marker sliding as bags, passengers and fuel icons fill in. Chips "WEIGHT", "BALANCE POINT", "TAKEOFF SPEED".

### domino_by_hand
VO: Domino two: the slow way. The math can be done by hand. But that can mean counting bags one by one, at every single gate.
Visual: Camera drops to the ramp: baggage carts and a belt loader; a stylised ramp worker with a clipboard, 2D tally marks ticking up. Counter "2".

### domino_gates
VO: Domino three: the gates. A plane that can't leave is still parked. So the next arriving plane has nowhere to go, and it waits on the taxiway.
Visual: Camera whips down the row of occupied gates to the taxiway, where arriving jets queue nose to tail. Red "GATE FULL" tags over each gate. **Hero moment 2:** high three-quarter view of the taxiway queue of jets facing a wall of full gates.

### domino_hub
VO: Domino four: the hub. To stop the pile-up, flights heading there can be held at their departure airports. That is called a ground stop.
Visual: Camera rises past the control tower into the sky; faint 2D route lines fan out from the airport and small plane icons along them turn amber and pause. Card "GROUND STOP".

### domino_crews
VO: Domino five: the clock. Pilots and flight attendants can only work a limited number of hours. When planes run late, crews can time out, and delays roll on long after the computer is fixed.
Visual: Camera descends to a terminal window where a stylised crew waits; a 2D duty clock above them runs down to zero. Counter "5"; a row of 2D dominoes tips across the top of the frame.

### resolution
VO: In this case, the system was back by Sunday afternoon. The airline has not said what caused it.
Visual: Camera glides back toward the first gate; the tablet overlay fills with numbers and turns green. Chip "RESTORED SUNDAY AFTERNOON · CAUSE NOT ANNOUNCED".

### takeaway
VO: Takeaway: check the app before you leave home, rebook in the app instead of the line, and keep essentials in your carry-on.
Visual: Jet bridge retracts and the first jet pushes back. 2D phone overlay with three ticks: "CHECK THE APP", "REBOOK IN THE APP", "ESSENTIALS IN CARRY-ON".

### outro
VO: What's the longest you've ever been stuck at an airport? Tell us in the comments, and send this to someone flying home this weekend. Because sometimes, a whole airline waits on one math problem.
Visual: Backout to the frame-1 view: the jet at its gate, row of parked jets behind, chip "ONE MISSING NUMBER" appears. Comment and share icons.
