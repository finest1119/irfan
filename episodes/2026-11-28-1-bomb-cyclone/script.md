## Voiceover script (9 beats)

### hook
VO: This Thanksgiving, a powerful storm swept up America's East Coast, strong enough to push New York's giant parade to Saturday. Forecasters warned it could deepen so fast, it might earn a dramatic name: bomb cyclone.
Visual: Frame 1: the seaside house on its bluff in bright hazy light, flag on the porch starting to flutter, a vast white comma of cloud filling the horizon. Card "US EAST COAST · THANKSGIVING 2026", chip "FORECASTERS: COULD BECOME A BOMB CYCLONE". **Hero moment 1:** the giant sunlit storm swirl over the sea behind the small house.

### question
VO: So what does a bomb cyclone actually feel like? Let's put you on the coast, in a storm like that.
Visual: Camera pushes to the porch. Title card "IF YOU WERE THERE: BOMB CYCLONE", small chip "ILLUSTRATION · A TYPICAL STORM".

### you_morning
VO: You're in a house by the sea. It's morning. Grey sky, light rain, nothing special. But the barometer on your wall is falling. Fast.
Visual: Close on a brass wall barometer by the porch door; the needle slides steadily left. 2D pressure readout ticking down with a small down-arrow. Light rain streaks, still bright.

### you_engine
VO: Here's what you can't see. Offshore, cold air from the land is crashing into warm, wet air over the ocean. That clash is fuel. And high above, the jet stream is pulling air up and away, like a vacuum over the storm.
Visual: Fast eased whip from the porch out over the sea and up: translucent blue arrows (cold, from land) meet orange arrows (warm, from ocean) and spiral upward; at the top a 2D ribbon labelled "JET STREAM" races across. Chips "COLD AIR", "WARM OCEAN AIR".

### you_pressure
VO: Air leaves the top faster than it can fill in below. So the pressure drops. When it falls far enough in just twenty-four hours, forecasters call it bombogenesis. Nothing explodes. It's simply a storm growing at full speed.
Visual: Camera looks straight down the storm's core from above; the swirl tightens and its centre sinks like a funnel. 2D gauge with a falling needle and a 24-hour dial sweeping once. Card "BOMBOGENESIS", optional small chip "ROUGHLY 24 MILLIBARS IN 24 HOURS (VARIES WITH LATITUDE)". **Hero moment 2:** top-down view of the tightening comma swirl, sunlit, with the coastline and the tiny house at the edge.

### you_wind
VO: Now you feel it. Air races toward that low. The wind doubles. Rain goes sideways. A storm like this could gust as hard as a hurricane, and it usually shoves the sea toward the shore.
Visual: Camera dives back to the beach, riding the wind arrows inland: flag snapping straight, rain streaks horizontal, dune grass flattened, waves stacking against the pier and running up the beach. Chips "WIND RUSHES TO THE LOW", "SEA PUSHED ASHORE". No people, no damage.

### you_cold
VO: Then the wind swings around. Cold air pours in behind the storm, and that rain could turn to snow.
Visual: Camera wraps around the house as the wind arrows swing from sea-side to land-side and turn blue; rain streaks soften into snowflakes; the sky behind the storm clears to bright cold blue.

### takeaway
VO: Takeaway: before a storm like this, charge your phone, tie down anything loose, and stay off the beach and out of flooded roads.
Visual: Porch in clear light: a phone on charge, a patio chair strapped down, a "ROAD FLOODED" sign glowing on the beach road. 2D checklist with three ticks: "CHARGE YOUR PHONE", "TIE DOWN LOOSE ITEMS", "STAY OFF THE BEACH AND FLOODED ROADS".

### outro
VO: Have you ever been through a bomb cyclone? Tell us where in the comments, and send this to someone on the East Coast. Because this Thanksgiving, forecasters were watching the pressure fall, and wondering if this storm would bomb.
Visual: Backout to the frame-1 view: the house on its bluff, the giant sunlit swirl on the horizon, barometer chip with a down-arrow. Comment and share icons.
