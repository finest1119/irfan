## General explainer (not event claims)
- Bombogenesis = a mid-latitude cyclone that rapidly intensifies; benchmark is a central-pressure fall of at least 24 millibars in 24 hours at 60° latitude, less at lower latitudes (NOAA: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/bombogenesis.html ; restated by Savannah Morning News and Asbury Park Press above).
- Fuel: cold continental air meeting warm, moist air over the ocean (often over the Gulf Stream); the jet stream removes air aloft so surface pressure falls; air rushing toward the low makes the wind; onshore wind pushes water toward the coast; cold air wraps in behind. Standard meteorology (NOAA page above; NWS JetStream: https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream).
- Strong bomb cyclones can produce hurricane-force gusts (general; worded "could").

- "35 millibars in 24 hours" (one opened source, a local forecast, not a measurement). The 24 mb threshold is NOT spoken (latitude-dependent); it is offered only as an optional chip with "varies with latitude".
