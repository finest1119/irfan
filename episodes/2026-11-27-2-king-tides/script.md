## Voiceover script (9 beats)

### hook
VO: This week, the Pacific Ocean climbed onto streets along America's West Coast. From Oregon to Southern California, tide gauges logged record or near-record water levels.
Visual: Frame 1: sunny waterfront street; a thin sheet of seawater slides over the seawall and spreads across the road, reflecting the palms. Card "US WEST COAST · NOV 2026", chip "RECORD OR NEAR-RECORD WATER LEVELS". **Hero moment 1:** water pouring over the seawall onto the bright street.

### question
VO: It's called a king tide. And most of what people believe about it is wrong.
Visual: Camera glides to the seawall edge, water level with the top. Title card "KING TIDE: MYTH VS REALITY".

### myth_wave
VO: Myth one: it's a giant wave. Reality: a king tide is slow. It is simply the highest high tide of the year, rising quietly over hours, when the moon, the sun and the Earth line up, and the moon swings in close.
Visual: Camera travels out along the pier beside a tide gauge post; the water creeps up its marks. Then a smooth tilt up to the big moon; a 2D line links Moon–Earth–Sun icons. Stamp "MYTH: GIANT WAVE" → "REALITY: A SLOW, HIGH TIDE".

### myth_storm
VO: Myth two: the storm causes it. Reality: the tide is the moon's work, and it's on the calendar years ahead. A storm only adds to it. Low pressure lets the sea bulge upward, and onshore wind piles water against the coast.
Visual: Camera pans from the moon across to the offshore storm bank; a 2D calendar page flips with a marked tide date. Under the clouds the sea surface domes up slightly; wind arrows push water toward shore. Label "ILLUSTRATION".

### myth_alone
VO: Myth three: the tide alone floods cities. Usually, it's just a nuisance. This time, forecasters say it stacked on top of a strong El Niño. Warm water takes up more room, so the ocean along the coast was already sitting higher.
Visual: Camera drops to the waterline by the pier: a cutaway of the sea glows warm orange and swells, lifting the surface. 2D stack on the gauge post, three blocks rising: "WARM OCEAN (EL NIÑO)", "KING TIDE", "STORM".

### stack
VO: Warm ocean. King tide. Storm. Each one adds a little. Together, they go over the wall.
Visual: Camera rides back in toward shore just above the water as the three stacked blocks reach the seawall top and the water spills over. **Hero moment 2:** low angle, the sea brimming exactly level with the seawall, moon huge behind.

### myth_over
VO: Myth four: once the storm passes, it's over. Reality: king tides come back. Forecasters expect another round around Christmas week, and again in late January.
Visual: Water drains back off the street; the moon arcs across the sky as 2D date chips appear: "CHRISTMAS WEEK", "LATE JANUARY".

### takeaway
VO: Takeaway: if you live or park near the water, check the tide tables, move your car to higher ground, and never drive through salt water on the road.
Visual: On the street, a small car backs away from the wet kerb up a gentle slope; phone-style 2D tide chart with a peak marked; a "ROAD FLOODED" sign glows.

### outro
VO: Have you ever seen the ocean come over the wall? Tell us where in the comments, and send this to someone who lives by the sea. Because this week, the Pacific came up the street, and it didn't need a giant wave.
Visual: Backout to the frame-1 view: the sheet of water sliding over the seawall onto the sunny street, moon above. Comment and share icons.
