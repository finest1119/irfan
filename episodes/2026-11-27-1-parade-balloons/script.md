## Voiceover script (9 beats)

### hook
VO: This Thanksgiving, the giant balloons are not flying over New York. The one hundredth Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade has been moved to Saturday, because of a storm.
Visual: Frame 1: wide view down the avenue. The giant balloon sits low over the street under a cargo net, lines taut, flags and autumn leaves streaming sideways. Card "NEW YORK · THANKSGIVING 2026", chip "PARADE MOVED TO SATURDAY 28 NOV".

### question
VO: This parade usually marches on through rain and cold. So why would wind stop it? Follow the questions.
Visual: Camera pushes slowly toward the balloon; rain streaks pass. Title card "WHY WOULD WIND STOP A PARADE?".

### why_balloons
VO: First question. Why not just march? Because of the balloons. A giant parade balloon is filled with helium and stands several stories tall. To the wind, that is not a toy. It is a sail.
Visual: The net slides off and the balloon rises on its lines beside the buildings; a 2D height bracket compares it with the floors next to it ("SEVERAL STORIES TALL"). A gust arrives and the balloon leans like a sail.

### why_not_hold
VO: But why can't the handlers just hold on? Each giant balloon has around eighty to a hundred of them. The trouble is the math. Double the wind speed, and the push is about four times stronger.
Visual: Camera tilts down the lines to the rows of small stylised handlers leaning back. Overlay: "80–100 HANDLERS". Wind arrows double in length; a 2D meter jumps "WIND ×2 → FORCE ×4". **Hero moment 1:** balloon straining sideways, every line taut.

### why_not_lower
VO: So why not fly them lower? In windy years, they have. But a city makes its own gusts. Tall buildings can squeeze the wind and fire it around corners.
Visual: Handlers haul the balloon lower. Camera whips along the avenue to the street corner, where animated wind ribbons squeeze between towers and curl around the corner, faster and brighter. Label "ILLUSTRATION".

### where_is_the_line
VO: So where is the line? New York City draws it. For the big balloons, the limits are twenty-three miles an hour sustained, and thirty-four in gusts. Officials said this storm's forecast gusts were well beyond that.
Visual: At the corner, a small weather mast with a spinning anemometer. 2D gauge with two marks: "23 MPH SUSTAINED", "34 MPH GUSTS"; the needle swings past both, chip "FORECAST: BEYOND THE LIMIT (officials)".

### what_now
VO: Only once before, in nineteen seventy-one, did weather keep the balloons down. This time, they were already being inflated. Officials said they would be let down, and readied again for Saturday.
Visual: Camera whips back up the avenue. The balloon settles gently to the street and softens under the net. Chip "1971: BALLOONS GROUNDED BY WEATHER", then "NEXT: SATURDAY, 8:30 AM".

### takeaway
VO: Takeaway: if wind can push around a balloon the size of a building, it can launch your trampoline, your patio umbrella and your yard inflatables. Tie them down before the storm.
Visual: Camera drifts past a small front garden on the avenue's park side: a patio umbrella, a trampoline and a small inflatable turkey tugging at their ropes; glowing tie-down straps click on.

### outro
VO: Which balloon is your all-time favorite? Tell us in the comments, and send this to someone who never misses the parade. Because this Thanksgiving, the wind decided: the giant balloons stay down.
Visual: Backout to the frame-1 wide view: balloon low under its net, leaves streaming. Comment and share icons. **Hero moment 2:** the netted giant balloon filling the avenue between the buildings.
