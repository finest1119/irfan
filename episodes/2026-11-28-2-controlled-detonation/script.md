## Voiceover script (10 beats)

### hook
VO: Wednesday. Nine A M. Southeast Iowa. The US Army blew up about five tons of its own explosives. On purpose.
Visual: Frame 1: wide, sunny prairie; the row of grass-covered bunkers ringed by tall barrier walls, water tanks beside them; a pale dust-and-foam puff rising softly behind the walls (abstract, no fire). Clock strip "WED 25 NOV · 9:00 AM CT". Card "IOWA, USA", chip "ABOUT FIVE TONS · CONTROLLED DETONATION". **Hero moment 1:** the ringed bunkers in bright morning light with the soft white puff above.

### question
VO: Why would an army destroy its own stockpile? Follow the clock.
Visual: The puff reverses into the ground as the clock strip spins back; camera begins its push toward the bunkers. Title card "WHY BLOW UP YOUR OWN EXPLOSIVES?"

### clock_march
VO: March. A routine inspection. The material is lead azide, and the Army said it had begun to crystallize inside its drums.
Visual: Clock strip "MARCH 2026". Camera glides through a bunker door to one plain drum on a pallet; a 2D magnifier shows pale needle-like crystals growing. Chip "THE ARMY: MATERIAL HAD CRYSTALLIZED".

### what_it_is
VO: Lead azide is a primary explosive. Usually, a tiny pinch sits inside a detonator, and its job is to set off a much bigger charge. So it is built to be sensitive. Crystals can make it more sensitive still: to friction, to shock, even to cold.
Visual: Camera pushes into a clean 3D cutaway of a pencil-sized detonator: a small glowing pellet at the tip labelled "PRIMARY EXPLOSIVE", a larger block behind it labelled "MAIN CHARGE"; a 2D spark hops from one to the other (diagram only). Icons "FRICTION", "SHOCK", "COLD". Label "ILLUSTRATION".

### the_trap
VO: That creates a trap. Explosives this touchy could go off just from being moved. So disposal teams usually stop trying to carry them away. They destroy them where they sit, at a moment they choose.
Visual: Camera pulls back out of the bunker: a forklift icon with a red 2D "DO NOT MOVE" tag over the drum; then a 2D stopwatch with a hand choosing the moment. Chip "TOO SENSITIVE TO MOVE → DESTROY IN PLACE".

### clock_days_before
VO: The days before. Crews raise barriers around the bunkers, and bring in foam and giant tanks of water, meant to soak up the blast and its dust.
Visual: Clock strip "THE DAYS BEFORE". Camera travels along the barrier ring as wall sections rise into place, white foam blocks stack up and a line of big blue tanks fills. Chips "BARRIERS", "FOAM", "WATER TANKS". **Hero moment 2:** low angle along the towering barrier wall and the row of blue water tanks in sunshine.

### clock_0900
VO: Nine A M. Detonation. Officials said the blast was driven down, into the ground.
Visual: Clock strip snaps to "9:00 AM CT". Camera rises above the ring: a single soft flash under the earth, the ground thumps, a white dust-and-foam puff lifts and thins (stylised, no fireball, no debris). 2D arrows point downward. Chip "OFFICIALS: BLAST DRIVEN INTO THE GROUND".

### clock_0915
VO: Nine fifteen. The Environmental Protection Agency said its air monitors showed no lead in the air. More than an hour later, it said the same. Soil and surface tests were still to come.
Visual: Clock strip "9:15 AM CT" then "MORE THAN AN HOUR LATER". Camera sweeps across the field to a slim air-monitor mast with a small spinning sensor; a 2D readout shows a flat line. Chips "EPA: NO LEAD IN THE AIR (EARLY READINGS)", then "SOIL AND SURFACE TESTS: STILL TO COME".

### takeaway
VO: Takeaway: if you ever find an old shell, old ammunition, or damaged fireworks, don't touch it and don't move it. Back away, and call the authorities.
Visual: Camera settles by the gravel road: a rusty generic shell shape half-hidden in the grass, a red 2D ring around it. 2D checklist: "DON'T TOUCH", "DON'T MOVE", "BACK AWAY AND CALL".

### outro
VO: Would you watch a controlled blast from a safe distance, or stay far away? Tell us in the comments, and send this to someone who loves knowing how things work. Because on Wednesday at nine A M, the Army decided the safest way to move five tons of explosives was not to move them at all.
Visual: Backout to the frame-1 view: the ringed bunkers on the sunny prairie, the soft white puff above, clock strip back at "9:00 AM CT". Comment and share icons.

## Risks / caveats

- **Politically and legally loaded story.** The script stays strictly technical: no contractor, no reason WHY the material degraded, no lawsuit, court, president, governors, plume maps or criticism. "The Army said" / "officials said" / "the EPA said" carry every event claim. The closing line attributes the judgment ("the Army decided").
- **"No lead in the air" is an early air reading**, attributed to the EPA and immediately followed by "soil and surface tests were still to come". Independent experts said it is too early to conclude. **Posting-day re-check required:** if sampling results or contamination reports are out, update that beat or hold the reel.
- **Real clock times:** only 9:00 AM and 9:15 AM Central (each 2+ opened sources). "March" (routine inspection) rests on PBS (opened) + Wikipedia; NBC and CBS confirm the crystallization but not the month, so if final QC wants it tighter, change the VO and strip to "Earlier this year". "The days before" is a relative label, not a reported date (the barriers, foam and tanks are verified; when they went up is not). "More than an hour later" is relative on purpose. Overlay must say "CT", and nothing else gets a clock time.
- "About five tons" only: pound figures conflict (9,500 / 9,700 / about 10,000). Mitigation is limited to barriers, foam and water tanks (each 2+ sources); no C-4, no crater size.
- The middle beats (`what_it_is`, `the_trap`) are a general explainer with usually/could; they do not say what would have happened in Iowa.
- Nothing graphic: the blast is a soft, stylised dust-and-foam puff behind walls, no fireball, no debris, no people nearby. Generic site: no real signage, unit insignia or logos. The detonator is a labelled diagram, not a how-to.
