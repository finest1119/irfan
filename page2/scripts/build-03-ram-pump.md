**Changed versus the proposal**
- Delivery spoken as "about a third of a gallon a minute" and "around five hundred gallons a day" (mid-range) in place of the range ¼–½ gal/min and 350–700 gal/day. The range stays as small text on screen.
- "About 90 % goes back to the creek" became "more than nine out of ten gallons" (the real share is about 94–96 %).
- No dollar figure for the water bill. The hook says only that the bill "just landed". The reel does not claim the pump replaces household water: it is creek water for the garden, and outdoor use is about 30 % of a typical home's water.
- SNAG 1 is the soft drive hose (rigid pipe needed); SNAG 2 is the burst fitting from the proposal's safety note. Beat 3 explains the pump on an animated diagram with a ghost drive line, so the hose in beat 4 is the first real drive line.
- Added the mission reason "save those watts" so the reel explains why he does not simply run an electric pump from Builds #1 and #2.

**Not verified / soft**
- The exact efficiency of a homemade ram at a 10 to 1 lift. Sources give 0.6 as typical and say homemade units do worse. I found no sourced figure for this exact case, hence "about" and "around".
- Friction loss in the long delivery pipe (roughly 300 ft of run up the meadow) is not included; at this tiny flow it should be small. My own estimate.
- The 8 gal/min drive flow is the proposal's figure. NC State lists minimum drive flows by pump size (2 gal/min for the smallest), so 8 fits a small ram. No pump size is spoken or shown.
- The "about 28 psi" static figure is my own hydrostatic arithmetic and is not on screen.

**Safety (covered by the caption line):** water hammer can burst weak pipe and fittings, so the reel shows pressure-rated parts and keeps Bolts and Torque clear of the pipe when the fitting fails. Taking creek water can need state water-rights approval. Untreated creek water is not for drinking. No sizes, valve settings or assembly steps are given.
