# Build #1 — Backyard wind turbine

**Page:** Self-Made Engineer · **Season 1 "Off-Grid Hollow"** · **Chip:** BOLTS · BUILD #1
**Title / hook line:** "Storm knocked out the power. So he built a wind turbine taller than his house"
**Narrator:** fixed Fenrir, natural American accent, third person. Bolts never speaks.
**Outfit:** `electrician` + fall-arrest harness (outfit chip: "TODAY: ELECTRICIAN BOLTS"). One outfit for the whole reel.
**Target length:** ~90 s at ~175 WPM (VO word count is in the final section).
**Lighting presets:** `dusk` (stormy, beats 1 and 9–11) and `day` (beats 2–8). Every preset change happens inside a travel move or snap, never on a hold. No night scene: dusk is the darkest.
**Earlier builds in the world:** none. This reel establishes house [H], street + mailbox [M], workshop + lean-to battery shed [S], back yard [Y], creek [C], ridge pad [R], goose house [T].
**Camera rule:** one continuous camera, never static, a move at each sentence start, whoosh about −20 dB under the VO.

Numbers are written the way the narrator says them. Only the lines tagged `VO:` are spoken.

## Beat 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:10, `dusk`, storm clearing)

VO: Storm rolls through, and boom, the whole street goes dark. Again. Last year the average American home sat without power for about eleven hours. Bolts was done waiting.

- **Move in:** opening 1 s drone-style whip from the horizon (water tower, wooded hills) down to the street at [M].
- **Hold:** slow drift along the sidewalk past 3–4 dark neighbor houses, wet asphalt, wooden utility poles, one sagging line. On "Bolts was done waiting": snap (0.3 s) to Bolts on the front porch of [H].
- **Overlays:** chip "BOLTS · BUILD #1" (top-left, small). Outfit chip "TODAY: ELECTRICIAN BOLTS" for 2 s. Big word captions mid-frame: "DARK." then "AGAIN." Label pinned to the street: "POWER OUT". Stat card: "≈ 11 HOURS without power per customer, 2024 (EIA)".
- **Bolts / Torque:** Bolts on the porch holding a dead flashlight, shakes it, hands on hips. Torque waddles past the mailbox with a head bob.
- **Assembly cue:** none. House windows dark, porch light off.

## Beat 2 — MISSION (0:10–0:19, whip into `day`)

VO: So he's building a wind turbine taller than his house. Fifty feet of steel, up on the windy ridge out back. And yes, he pulled the permit first.

- **Move in:** travel (2 s) up over the roof of [H], across the back yard [Y] and the creek [C], climbing the meadow slope to the ridge pad [R]. The dusk-to-day change is hidden in this move.
- **Hold:** crane up beside a ghosted outline of the finished tower.
- **Overlays:** chip MISSION. World-anchored height bars: "HOUSE ≈ 28 FT" and "TOWER 50 FT". Small label on the ridge: "WINDY RIDGE +80 FT". On the last sentence a paper stamp graphic "PERMIT ✔".
- **Bolts / Torque:** Bolts unrolls a plan on the tailgate of the red pickup, taps it with the carpenter's pencil, then puts the pencil back behind his ear.
- **Assembly cue:** survey stakes and string pop into the ground at the pad; the ghost tower outline pulses once.

## Beat 3 — HOW IT WORKS (0:19–0:32, `day`)

VO: Here's the deal. Wind pushes three blades, the blades spin a generator, and a tail keeps the whole thing pointed into the breeze. Power runs down a buried cable to a battery bank in the shed.

- **Move in:** snap (0.3 s) to an exploded view of the turbine head floating over the workbench area at [R].
- **Hold:** slow orbit around the head for the first sentence. Then travel (1.5 s) down the tower line and into a cut-away ground slice, following the buried cable downhill to the lean-to shed at [S]. End on a drift across the battery bank.
- **Overlays:** labels with glow on each part as it is named: "3 BLADES", "GENERATOR", "TAIL VANE". Animated wind arrows. In the ground slice: "BURIED CABLE". At the shed: HUD "BATTERY 12 %".
- **Bolts / Torque:** Bolts points at each part in turn, then walks toward the shed.
- **Assembly cue:** exploded parts slide together (hub, three blades, generator housing, tail) with small bounces. The cable trench fills itself in behind the camera. Battery boxes drop into the shed rack.

## Beat 4 — SNAG 1 (0:32–0:38, `day`)

VO: First snag: a fifty-foot tower doesn't stand itself up. And nobody's climbing that.

- **Move in:** travel (1.5 s) back up the slope to [R], arriving low along the tower lying flat in the grass.
- **Hold:** drift along the full length of the tower, base to tip.
- **Overlays:** chip SNAG. Dimension line drawn on the grass along the tower: "50 FT". A crossed-out ladder icon.
- **Bolts / Torque:** Bolts scratches his beard, looks from the tower tip to the sky and back.
- **Assembly cue:** tower sections bolt together flat on the ground; the turbine head mounts onto the tip while it is still down.

## Beat 5 — HACK 1 (0:38–0:48, `day`)

VO: The hack? A tilt-up tower. Build it flat on the ground, then crank it upright with a hand winch. Torque supervises. Torque also steals a washer.

- **Move in:** snap (0.3 s) to the hand winch and the hinge plate at the tower base.
- **Hold:** crane up, following the tower tip as it rises from flat to vertical. On "Torque supervises": snap (0.2 s) down to Torque at ground level.
- **Overlays:** chip HACK. Labels "HINGE", "GIN POLE", "GUY WIRES". Angle readout that climbs 0° to 90°.
- **Bolts / Torque:** Bolts cranks the winch (wrench clip), harness clipped to a ground anchor, well clear of the tower's fall line. Torque stands a safe distance away, then waddles off with a shiny washer in his beak. Bolts notices, shakes his head.
- **Assembly cue:** tower tilts up in one smooth motion, guy wires go taut one by one, base bolts spin in. The first gust turns the rotor.

## Beat 6 — SNAG 2 (0:48–0:54, `day`, sky darkens)

VO: Second snag: the next storm. Wind that strong can spin a small turbine to pieces.

- **Move in:** travel (1.5 s) up the tower to the rotor while dark clouds roll in over the ridge.
- **Hold:** orbit around the spinning rotor, trees on the ridge bending.
- **Overlays:** chip SNAG. HUD "WIND" gauge in mph climbing into the red zone (needle only, no furl speed printed). HUD "ROTOR RPM" needle climbing. Big word: "TOO FAST".
- **Bolts / Torque:** Bolts at the base holds his hard hat on with one hand. Torque is already inside the goose house, head poking out.
- **Assembly cue:** rotor blur increases; the tower sways slightly on its guy wires.

## Beat 7 — HACK 2 (0:54–1:04, `day`, stormy)

VO: So the tail does something sneaky. When the wind gets wild, it folds, and the blades swing sideways, out of the blast. It's called furling. The turbine basically ducks.

- **Move in:** snap (0.3 s) to the tail hinge behind the generator.
- **Hold:** slow orbit to a top-down view so the fold is easy to read.
- **Overlays:** chip HACK. Label "FURLING" with a curved arrow showing the rotor turning out of the wind. Wind arrows now slip past the rotor edge. HUD "ROTOR RPM" needle drops back to green.
- **Bolts / Torque:** Bolts watches from the base, slow nod.
- **Assembly cue:** tail folds on its hinge, rotor yaws edge-on to the wind, spin slows. As the gust passes the tail swings back and the rotor faces the wind again.

## Beat 8 — IT WORKS (1:04–1:21, `day`, clearing)

VO: And it works. Honest numbers: it's rated one and a half kilowatts, but that's the peak. Small turbines average a small slice of that. On a good windy site, figure around five kilowatt-hours a day. That won't run the whole house, but the fridge, the lights, and the phones? Covered.

- **Move in:** travel (2 s) from the rotor down the cable route to the lean-to shed at [S], then on through the kitchen window of [H].
- **Hold:** drift across the battery bank, then a slow push toward the fridge and a lamp.
- **Overlays:** chip IT WORKS. HUD "1.5 kW PEAK" with a bar that settles to a much smaller "AVERAGE" bar. Big number: "≈ 5 kWh / DAY" with small text "good windy site". HUD "BATTERY" climbing 12 % to 80 %. Icons that light up one at a time: fridge, light bulb, phone. Small text: "typical US home ≈ 28 kWh / day".
- **Bolts / Torque:** Bolts flips a switch at the shed, thumbs-up. Torque drops the stolen washer at his boots.
- **Assembly cue:** battery indicator lights step up; the fridge light comes on; the lamp glows.

## Beat 9 — CALLBACK to the hook (1:21–1:27, whip into `dusk`)

VO: So the next time the street goes dark, one porch light stays on. The neighbor has questions.

## Fact-check

|---|---|---|
| "Average American home sat without power for about eleven hours" last year | EIA: US electricity customers averaged about 11 hours of interruptions in 2024, about 80 % of it from major events (hurricanes Beryl, Helene, Milton). | Verified on the EIA page. **"Last year" is only right if 2024 is still the newest EIA year at posting time. If EIA has published 2025 data, update the number, or change the line to "in a recent year".** |
| Tower "fifty feet", taller than the house | DOE Small Wind Guidebook: keep the bottom of the rotor at least 30 ft above any obstacle within about 300–500 ft. A 2-story house is roughly 25–30 ft, so 50 ft on an open ridge pad is a believable minimum. | Verified (DOE). The 28 ft house bar is our model dimension, not a sourced fact. |
| "He pulled the permit first" | DOE: most zoning ordinances have a 35 ft height limit, so a taller tower usually needs a variance or permit. | Verified (DOE). |
| Three blades, generator, tail keeps it facing the wind | DOE guidebook: standard small upwind turbine layout with a tail vane. | Verified (DOE). |
| Tilt-up tower raised from the ground | DOE: tilt-down towers allow work at ground level, are usually used for turbines of 5 kW or smaller, and can be lowered ahead of hurricanes. | Verified (DOE). The hand winch and gin pole are shown as illustration only, with no rigging details. |
| Furling: the tail folds and the rotor turns out of the wind | DOE: most small turbines have automatic overspeed governing; furling is a passive system where the rotor folds up or around the tail vane. | Verified (DOE), plus general engineering literature seen only in search snippets. |
| "Rated one and a half kilowatts, but that's the peak" | Rated power is reached only at the rated wind speed. | Standard definition. |
| "Around five kilowatt-hours a day on a good windy site" | (a) PNNL Distributed Wind Market Report, 2024 edition: sampled small wind projects averaged a 13 % capacity factor in 2023, so 1.5 kW × 24 h × 0.13 ≈ 4.7 kWh/day. (b) DOE formula AEO = 0.01328 × D² × V³ with a 10 ft rotor at an 11 mph average ≈ 1,770 kWh/yr ≈ 4.8 kWh/day (12 mph ≈ 6.3 kWh/day). (c) DOE best case: a 1.5 kW turbine can supply about 300 kWh/month at a 14 mph average ≈ 10 kWh/day. | Verified with two methods. "Around 5" is a fair middle value. The 10 ft rotor diameter is my assumption for a 1.5 kW machine. |
| "Won't run the whole house" | EIA: average residential customer used 865 kWh/month in 2024 ≈ 28 kWh/day. 5 kWh/day is under a fifth of that. | Verified (EIA). |
| "Fridge, lights and phones covered" | A modern refrigerator uses roughly 1–2 kWh/day; LED lighting and phone charging are well under 1 kWh/day. | General knowledge, not fetched in this session. Low risk. |

**Changed versus the proposal**
- No furl wind speed is spoken or shown. The proposal row says "40+ mph storm"; I could not verify a furl speed with a US source, and it differs by turbine. The HUD shows a wind needle going into the red with no number.
- The proposal says "small turbines average ~15 % of that". The newest PNNL figure I found is 13 %, and older samples range widely, so the VO says "a small slice of that" with no percentage.
- Added the permit line ("he pulled the permit first"), because DOE says most zoning caps structures at 35 ft.
- Hook and payoff are set at stormy dusk, not night (proposal 4.2 item 7: no full night scenes).

**Not verified / soft**
- The 28 ft house height and the 10 ft rotor diameter are model assumptions.
- Appliance daily use figures come from general knowledge.

**Safety (already covered by the caption line):** no climbing, rigging or wiring steps are shown as instructions. Towers need county permits and possibly HOA approval; battery banks and house wiring are work for a licensed electrician.
