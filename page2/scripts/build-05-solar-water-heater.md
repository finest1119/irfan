**Changed versus the proposal**
- The proposal's "black pipe coil" became pipe in a climbing zig-zag. A flat spiral rises and falls on every turn and can air-lock, which stalls a thermosiphon; the weak natural flow needs a path that climbs all the way to the tank.
- Collector fixed at 4 ft × 6 ft (24 sq ft, inside the proposal's 20–30 sq ft) so the spoken size, the overlay and the gallons agree.
- Output spoken as "up to about thirty gallons" in place of the range 25–40 gal; tap temperature shown as 120 °F after the mixing valve, tank up to about 140 °F.
- "Steaming water on a cool morning" became a sunny-afternoon test with only a light wisp of steam. A simple tank is at its hottest in the afternoon, and heavy steam at 120 °F on a warm day would not be honest.
- Added the pressure figure (around 25 psi) from the hilltop tank, using the 65 ft height in the world model.
- SNAG 1 is the tank set too low (it has to sit above the collector); SNAG 2 is scalding, which puts the safety point inside the story.
- Added the spoken "honest note" on winter output and draining.
- No dollar figure and no "natural gas" wording, because the house runs on propane.

**Not verified / soft**
- The 30–40 % efficiency of a homemade pipe-coil collector and the resulting gallons. This is the weakest number in the reel, as the proposal's fact-check warned. Keep "up to about".
- Whether a single long zig-zag pipe gives enough natural flow. Commercial thermosiphon collectors use several parallel riser tubes, which have less resistance; a single serpentine works but flows slowly. General engineering knowledge, not fetched. This is one more reason the output is "up to".
- The DOE 18 % figure and the DOE "tank above the collector" wording were seen as search summaries only; energy.gov pages returned 404 or empty pages to the fetcher. Re-check with a readable DOE page at final QC.
- The shower arithmetic (8 minutes, two thirds hot).
- Flowing pressure at the shower after pipe friction; only the static figure is calculated.

**Safety (covered by the caption line):** solar-heated water can scald, so the reel shows a mixing valve and a relief valve. A tank fed from the hilltop under about 25 psi must be a pressure-rated tank with a temperature-and-pressure relief valve; an open or homemade tank cannot take that pressure. A full tank on a roof is heavy (a 40-gallon tank holds about 330 lb of water), so the roof has to be checked. Roof work needs fall protection (Bolts is clipped in for every roof shot). Drain before the first freeze. The water is untreated creek water: fine for an outdoor rinse, not for drinking, and warm stored water should be heated through regularly or drained to limit bacteria.
