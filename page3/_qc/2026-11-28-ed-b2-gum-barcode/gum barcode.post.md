## Voiceover script

Every beep at the checkout goes back to one pack of gum.
Those black bars aren't a picture. They're a twelve-digit number, written in stripes a machine can read.
The scanner sweeps a red laser across the stripes and reads that number back.
Here's what most people miss. The price usually isn't in the barcode. The store's computer looks the number up and sends the price back.
The very first scan at a real checkout was June 26, 1974, at a Marsh supermarket in Troy, Ohio. The item: a ten-pack of Juicy Fruit gum.
Now that same idea is on almost everything you buy.
