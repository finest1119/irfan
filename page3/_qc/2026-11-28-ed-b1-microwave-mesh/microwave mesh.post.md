# Why There's a Metal Mesh in Your Microwave Door

**Keyword / filename:** microwave mesh

## Facebook caption

That dotted screen in your microwave door is metal, and it's doing a big job. A magnetron fills the box with microwaves, the metal walls bounce them around until your food soaks them up, and the door's tiny holes are far too small for waves nearly five inches long to get through. Visible light is thousands of times smaller than the holes, so it slips right out and you can watch your food. Do you watch your food spin, or walk away?

## Voiceover script

There's a metal screen hidden in your microwave door. It's why you can watch your food cook.
Inside, a part called a magnetron makes microwaves, an invisible kind of radio wave.
Metal walls reflect them, so they bounce around until your food soaks them up.
They make the water molecules in your food vibrate, and that motion turns into heat.
Here's the trick. These microwaves are nearly five inches long. The holes are tiny, so almost none get out.
Visible light waves are thousands of times smaller than the holes, so light slips through. You see in, the microwaves stay in.
