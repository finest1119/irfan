## Voiceover script

The water starts at the bottom of a moka pot. What makes the coffee climb?
Before the pot goes on the stove, look for the little brass valve on its side. Filling just below that valve leaves a pocket of air above the water.
As the burner warms the water, trapped air and water vapor build pressure above its surface.
That pressure pushes down on the water. Watch the level fall as hot water rises inside the funnel.
Look closely at the basket. Water needs the tiny gaps between grains to get through. Spoon the coffee in loosely, because pressing it down makes that path harder for water to follow.
The water passes through the basket of grounds, picks up flavor, and climbs the chimney as coffee.
Just above the grounds sits a metal plate full of tiny holes. It holds most of the grounds in the basket while the liquid passes through into the chimney. Follow a single drop through one of those holes. It joins the other drops on the far side and flows up the hollow column.
Those first drops can spill into the upper chamber before the water below reaches a rolling boil.
Near the end of the brew, the water drops below the open end of the funnel. Gas can now rush up the same tube. That's the gurgle you hear from the pot.
Next time you pour a cup, picture that climb. How do you make your morning coffee? Tell us below.
