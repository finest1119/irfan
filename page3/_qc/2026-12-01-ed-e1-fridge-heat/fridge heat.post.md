# Your Fridge Doesn't Make Cold, It Moves Heat Into Your Kitchen

**Keyword / filename:** fridge heat

## Facebook caption

Your fridge doesn't really make cold. Inside, a liquid refrigerant runs through thin tubes and evaporates, soaking up heat from your food. A compressor squeezes that gas until it's hotter than your kitchen, and the coils on the back or underneath release the heat into the room. That's why an open fridge door won't cool the room: the fridge just runs harder and your kitchen ends up warmer. Be honest: how long do you stand at the open fridge hunting for a snack?

- iFixit, How Does a Refrigerator Work? (vapor-compression cycle: evaporator, compressor, condenser coils, expansion device): https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/How_a_Refrigerator_Works
