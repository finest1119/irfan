## Voiceover script

Noise-cancelling headphones don't just block outside sound. They actually fight sound with more sound.
Tiny microphones on the ear cups listen to the noise around you, like the hum of an air conditioner.
A chip inside makes a matching sound wave, flipped upside down, and plays it through the speakers.
When the two waves meet, the peaks fill the dips, so much of that hum cancels out.
Some headphones have a second microphone inside each cup. It picks up the noise that still reaches your ear, giving the chip a way to adjust the sound coming from the speaker.
That quieter spot is right by your ear. Someone sitting beside you still hears the air conditioner at full volume. The two sounds have to meet in the right place to cancel.
Here's the twist. It works best on steady, low hums. Voices are higher and keep changing, so more gets through.
The cushions block some noise even with the power off. If your glasses or some hair keep one cushion from sealing around your ear, adjusting that cup can make the room sound quieter.
Those outside microphones can do the opposite job, too. Switch to ambient sound mode on a model that has it, and the speakers play some of the room back to you.
