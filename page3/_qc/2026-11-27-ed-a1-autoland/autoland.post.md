## Voiceover script

Thick fog. The pilots can't see the runway. The plane lands anyway. Here's how.
Two invisible radio beams come up from the airport. The first, the localizer, tells the plane if it's left or right of the runway centerline.
The second, the glideslope, tells it if it's too high or too low, on a gentle slope of about three degrees down to the touchdown zone.
The autopilot locks onto both beams and flies the plane down them, correcting all the way.
On the strictest approaches, called Category Three, it can even touch down and hold the centerline, with visibility as low as about 300 feet.
But the pilots aren't passengers. They watch every second, ready to take over, and the plane, the crew and the runway all need special approval.
