# Fact-check for Why the Tray in Your Microwave Spins

| Claim in the reel or post | Source and evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two spoonfuls from the same microwaved bowl can have different temperatures. | Nebraska Extension describes cold spots within microwaved food. Delft's food demonstration shows different portions of one sample warming at different rates. [Nebraska Extension](https://food.unl.edu/article/microwave-safety/) · [Delft demonstration](https://interactivetextbooks.tudelft.nl/showthephysics/demos/demo49/demo49.html) | Supported. The hook gives a possible kitchen experience. |
| Microwaves reflect off metal oven walls. Overlapping waves can create stronger and weaker regions. | The FDA describes reflection inside the metal cavity. Delft demonstrates the field pattern and describes the stronger and weaker parts of a standing wave. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens) · [Delft](https://interactivetextbooks.tudelft.nl/showthephysics/demos/demo49/demo49.html) | Supported. The teal animation illustrates waves. It supplies no numerical field strength or hot-spot location. |
| Water in soup absorbs microwave energy. Regions absorbing more can heat faster. | The FDA explains how absorbed microwave energy heats food containing water. Delft shows localized heating in a food sample. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens) · [Delft](https://interactivetextbooks.tudelft.nl/showthephysics/demos/demo49/demo49.html) | Supported. The claim allows for differences in food composition and heat transport. |
| The spinning glass tray shown carries parts of the bowl through stronger and weaker regions, which helps even out heating. | DOE's microwave oven analysis describes manufacturers' use of turntables to move food through high-field regions. The scene depicts one glass-tray countertop model. [DOE, chapter 5, pp. 5-32 to 5-33](https://downloads.regulations.gov/EERE-2006-STD-0127-0070/attachment_7.pdf) | Supported for the type shown. The claim describes an improvement in uniformity. |
| The very center of this spinning tray stays in the same place in the oven. | This is a geometric inference from the fixed-axis turntable described by DOE and depicted in the cutaway. A point at radius zero travels a circle of circumference zero. The visual marker lies on the same vertical axis at the center of the bowl. [DOE](https://downloads.regulations.gov/EERE-2006-STD-0127-0070/attachment_7.pdf) | Confirmed for the depicted tray. The center's temperature is unspecified. |
| Stirring midway through heating can help even out the heat. | Nebraska Extension specifically recommends stirring midway to reduce cold spots. [Nebraska Extension](https://food.unl.edu/article/microwave-safety/) | Supported as a technique for more even heating. |

The cover questions and captions use these same claims. The final question and the Page comment ask for the viewer's own leftover.
