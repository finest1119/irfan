# Mildly Elsewhere · Story benchmark for the Malaysian pilots

**Use five distinct problems in an 80–85 second reel. Give one of them a fix that visibly fails, then repeat the same test.** Eight build Shorts with complete creator cross-post transcripts gave a median of **4 problems in 68 seconds**, **3.64 resolved cycles per minute** and **10 seconds per story beat**. At 83 seconds that density gives 5.0 problems. Match the finished house voices at about **186 words per finished minute for Kita Buat Dia** and **165 for Di Belakang Tabir**. **How to read the counts.** A problem is a concrete obstacle that gets a response. The initial mission, ordinary assembly and factual asides are excluded. Each number in brackets gives the corrective attempts for the corresponding problem, including its working fix. Count a retry when the same fault persists after a fix. Several parts installed before the next test count as one intervention. A working fix can expose a fresh problem. Cycles/min = distinct resolved problems × 60 ÷ YouTube runtime. A ≥ marks a lower bound.

**How to read the timing.** T = transcript of the creator’s matching TikTok post, indexed by TTAnon. D = creator’s YouTube description. R = timed third-party repost with the same distinctive story. For T, the opening column gives the first image evoked by the words, then the promise completed by the end of the sentence (≈3.3–5.5 s). The first 3 s are estimated from word position. The opening picture itself was unavailable. WPM ≈ transcript words × 60 ÷ YouTube seconds, including silence. Contractions and numerals each count as one token. T beat lengths are estimates from cumulative word position across the runtime. In order, they cover setup, each change of problem or retry, then payoff. The first beat can run beyond the opening hook. The matched cross-posts are within one second of their YouTube listings.

## Formula for a 75–90 s reel

| Time on an 83 s edit | Story action |
|---|---|
| 0–3 s | Let us see the contradiction and hear one short line. Spinning wheel, motionless fruit. Silent gutter, flowing outlet. |
| 3–14 s | Establish the goal and trace just enough of the mechanism to predict what should happen. |
| 14–30 s | Problem 1. Show a plausible fix, its failure, then the working fix. Keep the load or rainfall and the camera scale constant on the retest. |
| 30–40 / 40–50 / 50–60 / 60–70 s | Problems 2–5, about ten seconds each. Give every problem a different visible symptom and let the viewer see its fix work. |
| 70–83 s | Prove the result at the opening object. SME gets a physical gag, a glimpse of the next build and a viewer question. ED gets one late explanation and an observation question. |

**Writing budget.** At the median density, 75–90 s supports about 4.6–5.5 problems. Write **5 problems, 6 corrective attempts, 1 failed fix** for each pilot. One retry is the choice seen in the mine, silo and farm. Give ED its one failed option as a same-rain comparison inside the model. Keep the water as the subject.
