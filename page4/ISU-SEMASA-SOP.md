## The two-source rule

Verify **the issue and the mechanism separately**. For the issue, get an accountable primary source plus a genuine cross-check with independent evidence. Inspect the underlying dataset or bulletin for a figure attributed to a single issuing authority. Only that authority can issue its official number. A matching article quoting the same press release checks the transcription only. Find another series, field report, technical reference or independently observed local consequence to support the broader interpretation. For the mechanism, use two independent competent sources, including a manual, standards body, engineering agency or subject specialist. If evidence supports only part of a claim, say only that part. If sources conflict on location, timing, cause or units, hold the affected claim **(perlu semak)**.

## Hold, moderate, correct

- Skip party politics, race, religion and royalty disputes. Cover festive logistics through the mechanism. A parade date or road closure needs a local notice. Do not use ritual as a visual gag.
- Skip unverified viral allegations, named culprits, blame for a service failure, unofficial death tolls and screenshots without provenance. Investigate a generic engineering question separately. Never present Din or a generic building as an actual witness or site.
- For an active emergency, share the official source in the post if relevant. Keep safety decisions with the responsible authority. Avoid recreating victims, trespassing, hazardous repair instructions and operational details of sensitive infrastructure.
- Monitor comments after release. Hide or report threats, slurs and personal data. Answer good-faith questions with the source. If a material fact is wrong, pause queued variants, withdraw the misleading version if necessary, publish a visible BM correction and log what changed. A caption update alone cannot fix false spoken narration.
